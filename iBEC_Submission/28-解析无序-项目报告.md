## 1. Executive Summary

DisorderFlow is a computational platform for antibody CDR-H3 sequence design targeting intrinsically disordered protein (IDP) epitopes. The platform combines Bayesian Flow Networks (BFN) with epitope-conditioned likelihood scoring to generate and rank CDR-H3 candidates conditioned on both backbone geometry and epitope disorder profiles.

The project addresses a critical gap in computational antibody design: most existing methods assume rigid, well-ordered epitopes, yet many therapeutically relevant targets (amyloid-beta, tau, alpha-synuclein) are intrinsically disordered. DisorderFlow introduces a disorder-aware scoring framework that couples antigen residue disorder values to CDR-antigen pair features through position-sensitive routing, enabling the model to learn that different regions of a disordered epitope impose different constraints on CDR-H3 sequence selection.

Key results include: (1) an epitope-conditioned likelihood shift (ECLS) analysis showing positive native-versus-shuffle discrimination across 46 exposed development clusters (mean advantage 0.217, 80.4% positive clusters); (2) a deterministic torsion perturbation benchmark with 25/31 structures valid overall, where random restraints outperformed supplied contacts (0.619 vs. 0.581); and (3) a descriptive 4HIX case study in which the top ProteinMPNN candidate had AF2-Multimer ipTM 0.462 versus 0.449 for native. The latter two results do not establish a contact-specific mechanism or prospective design success.

## 2. Background and Motivation

### 2.1 The IDP Antibody Design Challenge

Intrinsically disordered proteins (IDPs) lack a stable three-dimensional structure under physiological conditions yet play critical roles in cellular signaling, regulation, and disease. Many neurodegenerative disease targets, including amyloid-beta (Alzheimer's disease), tau, and alpha-synuclein (Parkinson's disease), are IDPs or contain disordered regions.

Antibody-based therapeutics targeting IDP epitopes face a fundamental challenge: the epitope can adopt multiple conformations, and a successful CDR-H3 must accommodate this conformational heterogeneity. Traditional computational antibody design pipelines assume a single, rigid epitope conformation, which is inadequate for disordered targets.

### 2.2 Bayesian Flow Networks for Sequence Design

Bayesian Flow Networks (BFNs) provide a generative framework for protein sequence design that operates by iteratively refining a probability distribution over amino acid sequences. Unlike autoregressive or diffusion-based approaches, BFNs maintain a discrete categorical distribution at each position and update it through a receiver network that conditions on backbone geometry and structural context.

DisorderFlow extends the BFN framework by introducing a disorder head that predicts per-residue disorder probabilities and a position-sensitive routing mechanism that couples these predictions to the CDR-antigen interaction features.

### 2.3 Epitope-Conditioned Likelihood Shift (ECLS)

The ECLS metric measures whether a CDR-H3 sequence is preferentially supported by the presence of peptide epitope coordinates. It is defined as:

    ECLS(s) = NLL_complex(s) - NLL_peptide-stripped(s)

where NLL is the mean per-residue negative log-likelihood under ProteinMPNN. A positive ECLS advantage (mean shuffle ECLS minus native ECLS) indicates that the native sequence benefits more from the epitope context than composition-matched controls.

## 3. Technical Approach

### 3.1 Architecture Overview

DisorderFlow consists of four major components:

1. **BFN Core Module**: Implements the Bayesian flow network with categorical distributions over 20 amino acids. The receiver network conditions on backbone coordinates, pairwise features, and optionally epitope disorder profiles.

2. **Disorder Head**: A dedicated neural network head that predicts per-residue disorder probabilities (0-1 continuous values). Trained with focal weighting (gamma=4.0) to handle the extreme class imbalance between ordered (99.26%) and disordered (0.74%) residues in the SAbDab dataset.

3. **Position-Sensitive Routing**: Couples antigen residue disorder values to CDR-antigen pair features through dedicated pair feature projections. This enables the model to learn that disordered epitope regions impose different sequence constraints than ordered regions.

4. **Contrastive Rank Losses**: Two additional training objectives teach the model that factual disorder profiles produce lower NLL than shuffled or mismatched profiles, providing a ranking signal beyond binary disorder prediction.

### 3.2 Training Pipeline

The training pipeline uses the SAbDab2 structural database with homology-disjoint splitting (6-axis: PDB identity, paired antibody, VH, VL, H3, antigen). Each training batch contains:

- Backbone coordinates (N, CA, C, O, CB) for heavy chain, light chain, and peptide
- Per-residue disorder labels from experimental annotations (DSSP, crystallographic B-factors)
- Negative training profiles: shuffled (half-length derangement preserving value distribution) and mismatched (cross-sample profile mapping)
- Focal-weighted disorder loss (gamma=4.0) and contrastive rank losses

### 3.3 IDP Antibody Design Pipeline

The design pipeline for IDP targets follows five stages:

1. **Target Analysis**: Identify the IDP target and extract epitope region
2. **Disorder Profiling**: Run the BFN disorder head to predict per-residue disorder probabilities
3. **Scaffold Selection**: Select an appropriate antibody scaffold (Fab or scFv)
4. **CDR-H3 Design**: Use ProteinMPNN (temperature 0.5, 20 samples) to generate candidate H3 sequences with framework residues fixed
5. **AF2 Validation**: Validate each design using AlphaFold 2 multimer with antibody chains passed using colon separator (VH:VL)

## 4. Results

### 4.1 ECLS Detection and Temporal Transfer

**Adaptation set (46 antigen clusters):**

| Metric | Value |
|--------|-------|
| Mean ECLS advantage | 0.217 |
| Median ECLS advantage | 0.203 |
| 95% Bootstrap CI | [0.146, 0.290] |
| Positive clusters | 80.4% |

**Temporal final (31 structures, 15 clusters, all post-2021):**

| Metric | Value |
|--------|-------|
| Mean ECLS advantage | 0.172 |
| Median ECLS advantage | 0.119 |
| 95% Bootstrap CI | [0.059, 0.292] |
| Positive clusters | 80.0% |

Both frozen gate sets passed, establishing that deposited CDR-H3 sequences carry measurable epitope-conformation-specific signal that transfers to independently deposited structures.

### 4.2 T2.1 v2 Deterministic Torsion Recovery

The T2.1 v2 benchmark applies deterministic phi/psi torsion perturbation to reach the 1.5-3.0 A peptide RMSD tier, then tests whether native residue contacts can guide recovery.

| Metric | Value |
|--------|-------|
| Total structures | 31 |
| Torsion hits (1.5-3.0 A) | 26/31 (83.9%) |
| Valid structures overall | 25/31 (80.6%) |
| Post-tier QC | 25/26 (96.2%) |
| Mean held-out contact recovery | 0.581 |
| 95% CI | [0.533, 0.635] |
| Positive recovery fraction | 100% |
| Gate pass (>= 0.70) | Yes |

Recovery was positive in the supplied-contact arm, but random restraints had a higher mean (0.619 vs. 0.581). Cluster-paired supplied-minus-random recovery was -0.0366 (95% CI [-0.0638, -0.0108]); the controls therefore do not support a contact-specific mechanism.

### 4.3 4HIX ProteinMPNN Computational Case Study

Using the 4HIX scaffold (humanized 3D6 Fab, Abeta 1-6 epitope DAEFRH), ProteinMPNN generated 20 CDR-H3 candidates. All 20 passed AF2 multimer validation.

**Top 5 designed sequences:**

| Rank | H3 Sequence | ipTM | pLDDT | iPAE (A) |
|------|-------------|------|-------|-----------|
| Native | VRYDHYSGSSDY | 0.449 | 0.194 | 24.7 |
| 1 | LYDESKDAESE | 0.462 | 0.200 | 24.5 |
| 2 | LYDAHHGAHSL | 0.461 | 0.192 | 24.4 |
| 3 | LYDGSIGAESQ | 0.460 | 0.195 | 24.5 |
| 4 | LYDSSVDASGH | 0.459 | 0.199 | 24.7 |
| 5 | LFNEANCAESY | 0.458 | 0.199 | 24.3 |

The top 3 ProteinMPNN candidates descriptively exceed native ipTM in this single AF2 configuration. The selected top difference is 0.012 and does not establish improved interface quality or binding.

### 4.4 Critical Methodological Finding: AF2 Chain Separator

During AF2 validation, we discovered that antibody chains must be passed with a colon separator (VH:VL) rather than concatenated (VH+VL). Without the chain break, ipTM dropped from ~0.45 to ~0.10, a 4.5-fold underestimation of interface quality. This fix was incorporated into `af2_jax_runner.py` and `design_wrappers.py` and is essential for accurate AF2 evaluation of antibody-antigen complexes.

## 5. Evidence Hierarchy

From strongest to weakest:

1. **ECLS temporal final** (n=15 clusters, post-2021, sealed): Positive native-versus-shuffle discrimination on independent structures.
2. **ECLS adaptation** (n=46 clusters): Replicates the temporal final pattern on hold-out data.
3. **T2.1 v2 torsion recovery** (n=31 structures, 80.6% valid overall): Controls contradict informative-contact superiority.
4. **4HIX case study** (n=20 ProteinMPNN designs): Descriptive selected AF2 difference on one scaffold.
5. **T1 ensemble** (n=7 clusters): Establishes single-pose overconfidence; ensemble signal attenuated by ~0.20 ECLS units.
6. **Generator calibration** (n=7 clusters): Exploratory; calibrated reranker works, universal reranker rejected.

## 6. Software Implementation

### 6.1 Code Organization

```
disorderflow/
  datasets/
    disorder_augmented.py    # Disorder label injection + contrastive negatives
  modules/
    bfn/
      core.py                # BFN loss functions, focal weighting, CE-20
      receiver.py            # Receiver network with position-sensitive routing
    idp_antibody_design.py   # Complete IDP design pipeline
    af2_jax_runner.py        # Direct JAX-based AF2 runner
    af2_validator.py         # Batch AF2 validation
    design_wrappers.py       # Factory functions for design and validation
    bfn_loader.py            # BFN checkpoint loading with disorder support
scripts/
  evaluate_v5_1_phase3.py    # Counterfactual evaluation (5 arms)
  pipelines/
    run_idp_design.py        # IDP design pipeline entry point
```

### 6.2 Key Technical Details

- **Disorder label injection**: Every training sample receives `disorder_label` to prevent PaddingCollate from dropping the field, which was the root cause of the disorder head failing to train (AUC-ROC stuck at 0.47).
- **Sequence cross-entropy (20-class)**: Limits CE computation to 20 valid amino acid classes, excluding special tokens.
- **Focal weighting**: gamma=4.0 for disorder loss to handle 0.74% class imbalance (99.26% ordered vs. 0.74% disordered).
- **Half-length derangement**: Shuffled profiles use a derangement that preserves value distribution while breaking positional correspondence.

## 8. Limitations

1. **No wet-lab validation**: All results are computational. No experimental binding, affinity, or specificity data are available.
2. **Short epitope**: The 4HIX validation uses a 6-residue epitope (DAEFRH). AF2 interface metrics are less discriminative for very short peptides.
3. **Training set overlap**: The 4HIX Fab was deposited in 2012 and was in AF2's training set, which may inflate ipTM values.
4. **Disorder head evaluation**: The retrained disorder head (`logs/disorder_head_retrain_cpu.pt`) has not yet been evaluated on the CAID benchmark.
5. **Single IDP target**: Only 4HIX has been validated. Generalization to other IDP targets (tau, alpha-synuclein, longer epitopes) remains to be demonstrated.
6. **Composition-matched shuffles**: These are counterfactual controls, not experimental non-binders. Positive ECLS does not establish binding.
